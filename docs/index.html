How it works

It answers a question your devices ask constantly

One device covers everything on your Wi-Fi. No apps, no settings on your phone.

Every time a device opens a page it first asks “where does this website live?” Normally your internet provider answers. AdFree answers instead — and for the roughly 490,000 addresses known to serve ads and tracking, it simply declines. The ad has nowhere to load from, so the page arrives without it.

What to buy

One part, about $10

AdFree is free software. You supply the hardware it runs on: a small bare circuit board about the size of a stick of gum, with a USB-C socket on one end. It's the same kind of chip that's already inside smart plugs and Wi-Fi bulbs.

Not these

These look almost identical and cost the same, but AdFree will not run on them:

  • ✕Plain ESP32 or ESP32-WROOM — older chip, too little memory.
  • ✕ESP32-C3 or ESP32-S2 — wrong family.
  • ✕ESP32-S3 with no PSRAM — listed as N8, N16, or with no “R” number at all. The R8 is what holds the block list.

If a listing doesn't clearly say 8 MB PSRAM or R8, assume it hasn't got it.

Also needed

  • ✓A USB-C cable that carries data. Many cheap cables only charge — if the board never shows up, this is almost always why.
  • ✓Any USB power adapter. An old phone charger is perfect.
  • ✓A computer running Chrome, Edge or Opera, for the one-time setup only.

You do not need

  • ✕Soldering, wiring, or a case
  • ✕A Raspberry Pi
  • ✕Any coding or command line
  • ✕An account, subscription or app

Setup

Four steps once the board arrives

Nothing is installed on your computer — your browser writes to the board directly over USB.

Step 1 · Put the software on the board

Plug the board into this computer with the USB cable, then press the button.

A list will pop up. Here's what to click:

USB JTAG/serial debug unit (COM5)this one
USB Serial Device (COM7)or this
Communications Port (COM1)not this

Pick the entry with USB in its name — that's your board. Anything called Communications Port is part of your PC, not the board. If the list is empty or has only Communications Port, your cable is charge-only — swap it for a different one.

This browser can't install to a board.
Open this page in Chrome, Edge or Opera on a desktop or laptop. Safari, Firefox and phone browsers don't have the USB feature this needs.

Free and open source. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Move it to a charger

Unplug it from your computer and plug it into any USB power adapter near your router. It never needs your computer again.

Tell it your Wi-Fi, from your phone

A new network starting with ADFREE- appears in your phone's Wi-Fi list. Join it. Its password is unique to your board and is shown in the installer's Logs & Console right after step 1 — write it down then. A page opens by itself: enter your home Wi-Fi and choose a password for AdFree's own settings page.

Point your router at it

Open http://adblock.local to see the board's address, then change one setting in your router so the whole house uses it. Instructions for your router →

Before you buy

The honest version

AdFree works well, but it isn't magic, and it changes the internet for everyone in the house. Read this before you spend anything.

What it removes

  • ✓Banner and pop-up ads on websites
  • ✓Tracking and analytics scripts
  • ✓Ads inside many free phone apps and games
  • ✓Smart-TV and streaming-stick tracking
  • ✓Known scam and malware sites

What it cannot remove

  • ✕YouTube ads. They come from the same servers as the videos, so blocking them would block YouTube itself.
  • ✕Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and Spotify in-app ads — same reason.
  • ✕Netflix, Hulu and Twitch ad breaks.
  • ✕Sponsor reads inside videos and podcasts.

No device of this kind can. If something claims otherwise, it isn't working the way this does.

If it's unplugged, browsing stops

Once your router relies on AdFree, a dead or unplugged board means nobody can browse until it's back — or until you undo one router setting. Five minutes to fix, but you have to know that's the cause. Keep the undo steps handy.

It affects everyone in the house

Including guests and anyone working from home. Occasionally a site misbehaves without its ads and you become the person who gets asked about it. Tell the people you live with first.

It isn't privacy or security software

Your internet provider can still see which sites you visit. AdFree is not a VPN and not antivirus, and it won't stop you typing a password into a fake site.

Some devices quietly ignore it
These aren't faults in AdFree — they're devices choosing to bypass your router's settings. Chrome and Firefox's “secure DNS” sends requests straight past it; switching that off in the browser restores blocking. Chromecasts, Google Home and some smart TVs have their DNS fixed at the factory and ignore your router entirely. iCloud Private Relay and any VPN bypass it on that device. And phones are only covered while they're on your Wi-Fi, not on mobile data.
Wouldn't a browser extension be easier?
Honestly, yes — if you only care about one computer. uBlock Origin is free, takes ten seconds, and blocks more on that machine than AdFree can, including some YouTube ads. AdFree earns its place when you want to cover the things you can't install an extension on: smart TVs, phones, tablets, consoles and guests' devices.

The router step

Changing one setting on your router

The only fiddly part, and the one step no software can do for you — routers have no standard way of being configured remotely.

If your router won't let you change it

Some providers lock that setting. You can set it on individual devices instead — which is also a good way to try AdFree on one phone before committing the whole house.

iPhone / iPad

Settings → Wi-Fi → tap the ⓘ beside your network → Configure DNS → Manual → remove what's there → add the board's address → Save.

Android

Settings → Wi-Fi → hold your network → Modify → Advanced → IP settings → Static → set DNS 1 to the board's address.

Windows

Settings → Network & Internet → Wi-Fi → Hardware properties → DNS server assignment → Edit → Manual → IPv4 on → enter the address.

macOS

System Settings → Network → Wi-Fi → Details → DNS → press + → enter the board's address → OK → Apply.

If something goes wrong

Nothing here is permanent

Save this to your phone. If AdFree is ever the problem, you may not have working internet to look it up.

One website is broken

Open http://adblock.local and press Pause. If the site works now, add it to the allow list and unpause. If it still doesn't, AdFree wasn't the cause.

Nothing loads at all

Look at the small light on the board. Red or dark means it lost Wi-Fi or power. Unplug it, plug it back in, wait a minute.

Undo everything — the emergency exit

Log into your router, find the DNS setting you changed, and put it back to Automatic or Obtain from ISP. Save and restart the router. Your internet is exactly as it was, and no device needs touching.

Start over completely

Hold the board's BOOT button while plugging it in, then install again from the button above. That wipes every setting.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need to be technical?
No, but you do need to follow instructions carefully and log into your router once. If “log into your router” makes you uneasy, this will be frustrating — a browser extension is a better fit.
Will it slow down my internet?
No. It answers address lookups on your own network, usually faster than your provider does, and it never touches the content of pages.
Does it cost anything after I buy the board?
Nothing. No subscription, no account, no company involved. The software is free and open source and it uses roughly 40 cents of electricity a year.
Can I buy one already made?
Nobody sells a finished AdFree unit. You buy the bare board and this page turns it into one — that's why it's $10 and not $100.
What if I move house?
Open its settings page and enter the new Wi-Fi before you unplug it. If you forget, it creates its ADFREE- network again so you can set it up from a phone.
The Install button does nothing.
It needs Chrome, Edge or Opera on a desktop or laptop. If you're already using one, try a different USB-C cable — cheap cables often carry power but no data, and the board won't appear in the list at all.
